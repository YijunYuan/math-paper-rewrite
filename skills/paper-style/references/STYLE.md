# Default mathematical writing style

Adapted from Pharos's default STYLE.md. Use as a default, subordinate to explicit author instructions and venue requirements.

## Shape
Write an abstract that first states the proved result in language a non-specialist in the subfield can follow, then names its mechanism. Two to four result sentences and one mechanism sentence are a useful starting point, not a fixed quota.
Build an introduction from: precise background and problem; relevant prior results and their limitations; the actual main result; key ideas and their relation to prior work; a short roadmap where useful.
State standing notation once, in the introduction for a short paper or a setup section when needed. Place technical sections in proof-dependency order, usually leading to the main theorem's proof. Include a mechanism-revealing alternative proof or interpretation only when the manuscript supports it.

## Introduction
Define the objects needed to understand the main result. Explain significance concretely and briefly.
Describe prior work by what it proves, for which cases, and with which limitations, citing the relevant sources. Select literature by relevance and reliability, not author status, journal prestige, or an arbitrary publication-age cutoff.
State the main theorem with its actual hypotheses and conclusion. Explain what its hypotheses mean and what its conclusion adds. Give a concrete instance only when it is checked and useful.
Describe the observation, construction, imported theorem, or adapted method that overcomes the stated obstacle. Do not claim novelty beyond the evidence inspected.
Write the roadmap by section function, not merely by titles.

## Technical exposition
Open a section by recalling the needed setting and stating its goal.
Define terms before use. Give lemmas a clear role in reaching the section's result.
After an important theorem, explain its mathematical meaning when that adds information rather than repeating the formula.
For a long proof, outline its mechanisms and state what each phase establishes. Explain how cases or partial results assemble the conclusion.
Do not use “clearly,” “obviously,” or “similarly” to hide an obligation. Include the short derivation or a precise applicable citation.

## Voice and notation
Prefer direct declarative sentences, present tense for mathematics, and a consistent authorial voice; first-person plural is the default when the author has no preference.
Develop one main idea per paragraph. Begin with its purpose or assertion, then supply the reasoning.
Use the precise technical term consistently. Avoid decorative synonyms for defined objects.
Delete generic significance claims, repetitive summaries, artificial contrasts, and empty transitions. Preserve qualifications and logical detail.
Display expressions whose size or logical structure would make inline reading difficult; number equations that are referenced.
Use conventional theorem names or attribution in bracketed titles, rather than a miniature abstract.
Preserve the author's template, bibliography conventions, and meaningful formatting. Do not impose a specific font, table of contents, or appendix.

## Attribution
Keep contribution and disclosure claims grounded in supplied records. Using this skill does not mean Pharos generated or verified the manuscript.
